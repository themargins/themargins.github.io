---
title: The Lantern Within
description: On setting down other people's dreams and walking by your own light.
date: February 14, 2025
category: Poem
---

::: after
after [Khalil Gibran](/#the-prophet)
:::

Once, I walked the path of many feet, treading the dust of dreams not my own. I mistook the shimmer of distant stars for the fire of my own soul, and in chasing them, I forgot the glow that lay waiting in the quiet chambers of my heart.<span class="marginnote">Gibran is my favorite poet, and this was written in the spirit of *The Prophet*.</span>

I saw men toil for gold and I toiled with them, though my hands longed to shape the wind. I heard voices praising high towers, and so I climbed, though my spirit yearned for the vastness of the valley below. And when I reached the heights they called sacred, I found them empty, the echoes of my own longing lost among their stones.

Then a whisper, softer than dawn's breath, rose from within me: *You are neither the weight of another's burden nor the shadow of another's light. You were not born to follow a mirage, but to set forth with your own flame, no matter how small.*

And so, I turned inward and found a lantern unlit. With hands that once sought approval, I kindled its wick. With breath that once carried the weight of the world, I fed its glow. And as it burned, it did not scorch, nor did it blind—it simply shone.

I walked again, but not upon the path of many. My feet traced no road, yet each step was firm. And those who passed did not follow, nor did they turn away, but watched, and some too lit their own lanterns, until the night was no longer dark, but alive with a quiet constellation of souls.

For the way is not to be found in another's longing. The way is the light you bear, the fire you tend—not to consume, but to guide, not to burn, but to warm. Walk not in the footsteps of ghosts, nor in the shadows of giants, but in the steady glow of your own becoming.

And as you go, laugh at the wind, speak with the stones, dance with the rain. For to live truly is to bear both gravity and grace, to walk with purpose yet float with wonder—to be the flame, and the feather upon its heat.
