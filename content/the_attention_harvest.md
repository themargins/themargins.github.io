## What I said tonight (raw)

- These large companies are farming attention to convert it into profit. It feels like they're stealing the life force from people.
- I see it with my own dad: hours and hours on Facebook shorts, a lot of it AI-generated media.
- I see it in myself too: so many hours thrown into YouTube and consuming rather than building.
- Ever since I was little I've been a tinkerer. Taking things apart, putting them back together in new ways, devising solutions. A voracious reader who couldn't put books down.
- At 28, half way to 29, I feel like I've dulled. Turned greyscale. No longer following that child-like curiosity.
- I want to do something about it.

## Threads worth pulling

- The counterfeit: the feed gives curiosity its reward (the "oh, that's how it works" hit) without the friction that made tinkering nourishing.
- Not a willpower problem: one person vs. thousands of experiments designed to keep you there. AI filler makes content close to free to produce.
- Dad, without condescension: what is the scrolling filling for him? Loneliness, tiredness, a need to switch off? People rarely drop a habit because it's bad; they drop it when something more nourishing shows up.
- "Life force": be concrete. What exactly gets taken? Hours, yes, but also the colour, the attention I used to give to things.
- The kid with the broken radio vs. me with the phone.
- Connects to [Autopilot](autopilot.html): the fog folding back in, "and I let it."
- This site as a small counter-example: slow, no feed, no algorithm, made by a person.

## Questions I haven't answered yet

- Am I angry at the companies, or at myself, or both?
- What would "doing something about it" actually look like for me: building, writing, or closer to home?
- What did a good, un-harvested day look like as a kid? Can I describe one?
